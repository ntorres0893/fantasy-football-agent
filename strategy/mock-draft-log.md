# 🎓 Mock Draft Log — Lessons Learned

> Every mock gets logged here: what happened, what worked, what to fix. Read the
> **"Standing lessons"** section at the top of every future mock AND before the
> real draft on 9/3 — it's the accumulated coaching notes.

---

## 📌 Standing lessons (carry into every future mock + the real draft)

1. **When you pick 10th, the back-to-back turn (10&11, 30&31, 50&51...) is your
   superpower.** No one drafts between your two picks — treat each turn as "grab
   the best RB AND the best WR still there," not one pick at a time.
2. **Elite talent slides further than you'd expect.** Justin Jefferson (top-5
   overall) fell to pick 10 in Mock #1 because other teams reached for RB. Don't
   assume a stud is gone — check the board before panic-picking a lesser player.
3. **QB value shows up later than you think.** Only 1 QB was gone by pick 50 in
   Mock #1 — an elite dual-threat QB (Lamar/Daniels) falling to Round 5 is a real
   value grab, even though our default plan says "wait to R6-9." Stay flexible:
   if a top-2 QB is *still* there past your planned window, that's a signal to
   take him, not a reason to keep waiting on principle.
4. **WR depth needs active management.** In Mock #1 the roster ended thin at WR
   (only 2-3 rosterable) because RB/QB/TE picks crowded out mid-round WR grabs.
   Actively count "how many startable WRs do I have" every few rounds — don't
   let it slide just because RB feels more urgent.
5. **Check bye weeks as you draft, not after.** Mock #1 landed two bye-week
   collisions (wk 11: K.Williams + Kraft; wk 13: Taylor + G.Wilson) that went
   unnoticed mid-draft. When two rostered starters share a bye, that's a
   one-week hole — worth a tiebreaker nudge between similarly-ranked players.
6. **D/ST and K genuinely can wait** — correctly left empty until the very end
   in Mock #1. Keep doing that; don't let a "shiny" kicker tempt an early pick.
7. **Feed picks in batches during mocks, keep my replies to ~2-3 lines during
   live picks** — full breakdowns happen after, not mid-draft, so we can move
   fast when picks are flying.

## Mock #1 — 2026-08-2X · Beginner 10-Team H2H Points PPR · Slot 10

**Final roster:**
QB: Lamar Jackson (bye 13) · RB: Jonathan Taylor (13), Kyren Williams (11) ·
WR: Justin Jefferson (6), Garrett Wilson (13) · TE: Tucker Kraft (11) ·
FLEX: D'Andre Swift (10) · D/ST: empty · K: empty ·
Bench: M. Pittman Jr. (WR, 9), J. Brooks (RB, 5), J. Warren (RB, 9),
R. White (RB, 7), T. Hockenson (TE, 9)

**What went well:**
- Picks 10 & 11: Jefferson (WR, fell to us) + Jonathan Taylor (RB) — ideal
  best-player-available outcome for the slot.
- Tucker Kraft in Round 6 — exactly the "value TE" target we'd flagged pre-draft.
- Correctly waited on D/ST and K the whole draft.
- Kept pace well feeding picks in batches once we found a rhythm.

**What to improve next time:**
- WR corps ended thin (Jefferson, G.Wilson, Pittman) — should have prioritized
  a 3rd/4th WR earlier instead of doubling up more on RB depth late.
- Didn't flag the wk11/wk13 bye-week bunching in real time — build a habit of a
  quick bye-check every 2-3 rounds.
- Late rounds got messy/rushed ("we kind of broke down towards the end") — for
  the next mock, keep the same fast batch-feeding pace all the way to the final
  pick instead of losing structure late.
- Some rounds had inconsistent pick-count tracking (had to correct turn math
  live: 10&11 → 30&31 → 50&51..., not 10→20). Confirm turn math with the
  manager once at the start of a mock, then stick to it silently.

picks 1-11), `data/watch-list.md` (positional tiers used throughout).
